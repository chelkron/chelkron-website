Many growing businesses still run on free email addresses: *yourbusiness2024@gmail.com* on the business card, and staff using their personal accounts for customers. It works, until it doesn't. Here's why moving to email on your own domain, such as *sales@yourbusiness.com*, is one of the cheapest upgrades a business can make.

## Customers trust it more

When a quotation arrives from *ada@okaforfoods.com*, it looks like it comes from a real company. When it comes from *okaforfoods.lagos@gmail.com*, some customers will wonder. A proper address tells people you're established and here to stay. That matters even more when you're asking someone to pay an invoice.

It also protects your customers. Anyone can create a free address that looks like yours. Nobody else can send from your own domain once it's set up properly.

## Your emails land in the inbox, not spam

Email providers decide whether to trust a message partly by checking the domain it comes from. With your own domain you can publish three records that prove your emails are genuine:

- **SPF** lists the servers allowed to send email for your domain.
- **DKIM** adds a digital signature to every message you send.
- **DMARC** tells other providers what to do with emails that fail those checks.

## The email belongs to the business, not the person

When a staff member leaves, what happens to the customers who only know their personal email address? With your own domain, every address belongs to the business:

- You can **reset the password** or close the mailbox the day someone leaves.
- You can **hand their mailbox over** to whoever takes their place, so no customer email is lost.
- You keep the **history**: quotations, agreements and conversations stay with the company.

## One person, many addresses

You don't need a mailbox for every purpose. On plans that include them, **aliases** let addresses like *info@*, *sales@* and *support@* deliver to the right people's inboxes. A small team can look organised from day one, and you can change who receives what as you grow.
