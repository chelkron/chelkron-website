When you first move your business onto software, it's tempting to give everyone access to everything. It's quick, nobody gets stuck, and you trust your team. But as you grow, some information needs a smaller audience: salaries, company revenue, a disciplinary meeting, the full customer list.

Good permissions aren't about distrust. They protect your staff's privacy, keep sensitive figures in the right hands, and limit the damage if a phone is lost or an account is misused.

## Start from "only what they need"

The simplest rule is to give each person access to what their job needs, and nothing more. In practice that means:

- **Everyone** sees their own work: their tasks, attendance, leave, payslips and appraisals.
- **Managers** also see the work they hand out and review: the tasks they assigned, their department's leave requests and reports.
- **Administrators** see and manage everything, and should be as few as possible.

## The permissions in Chelkron Workforce

Workforce lets you switch on extra access for each person under **Management → Staff**. Here's what each permission does and who usually needs it:

- **Add tasks.** Create tasks and give them to others. For supervisors and team leads.
- **Delete tasks.** Remove tasks from the queue. Usually only one or two people.
- **Schedule meetings.** Put meetings on the company calendar.
- **View all reports.** Read every staff and department report, not just your own. For managers and leadership.
- **Comment on reports.** Reply to the reports staff submit, for whoever reviews them.
- **Add and edit leads.** Work with customers and deals in the CRM. For your sales team.
- **See business figures.** Revenue, pipeline, targets, the AI business insight, CRM totals and every department's measures. Keep this for leadership and senior managers.
- **Manage staff.** Add and edit staff accounts. Staff managers can only give out permissions they hold themselves, and can't change administrators or see salaries.

Without **See business figures** or **Add and edit leads**, a team member sees only the customers assigned to them. Meetings work the same way: people see whole-team meetings, meetings they set up, and meetings they're invited to, so a private meeting stays private.

## Five habits that keep access right

1. **Keep administrators to a minimum.** Two is usually enough: you, and one trusted backup.
2. **Review permissions when someone's role changes.** A promotion should add access, and a move to another team should remove what they no longer need.
3. **Remove leavers on their last day.** In Workforce, removing someone signs them out straight away.
4. **Turn on two-step sign-in** for administrators, so a stolen password alone isn't enough.
5. **Check what people can actually see.** Every so often, ask a team member to show you their dashboard. You'll quickly spot anything that shouldn't be there.

## When in doubt, start small

It's easy to add a permission when someone needs it, and awkward to take one away after they've seen something they shouldn't. Start with less, and add access as each person's role grows.
